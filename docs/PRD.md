# Product Requirements Document

**Document ID:** SOS-PRD-001  
**Version:** 1.0 (proposed baseline)  
**Status:** Draft for product and technical review  
**Product:** STATESMAN OS — A Practice System for Public Leadership  
**Initial market:** Private, individual use; Guyana-first scenario content, extensible internationally  
**Primary release:** Mobile-responsive web application (private account)  
**Product owner:** To be assigned  
**Prepared:** 20 October 2026

> **North star:** Turn ambition into tested judgment, ethical service, and verifiable results.

## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Product definition

STATESMAN OS is a **private decision-training and public-service evidence platform** for aspiring civic and institutional leaders. It presents users with realistic, sourced governance challenges; requires them to make and defend decisions under uncertainty; challenges their reasoning through different professional perspectives; and converts observed weaknesses into deliberate practice. Future versions may support independently verified public-service records and responsible long-term leadership planning.

**The MVP is not a political campaign tool, an election predictor, a governance certification, or an AI executive adviser for live emergencies.** Its first and most important job is to develop the habit of making difficult decisions, recording reasoning, receiving challenge, and improving through evidence.

### 1.2 Product problem

Leadership education often measures course completion or theoretical knowledge, while serious executive performance depends on navigating ambiguity, constrained resources, lawful authority, diverse stakeholders, unintended consequences, and accountability. Users need a repeatable practice environment that documents *how* they reason and whether that reasoning improves, rather than offering motivational dashboards or superficial personality quizzes.

### 1.3 Hypothesis and intended outcome

**Product hypothesis:** A structured, evidence-grounded decision practice, combined with challenge, reflection, and corrective actions, can improve the clarity, defensibility, and calibration of a user's governance reasoning over time.

**Outcome sought:** Users demonstrate better quality of reasoning across comparable scenarios and complete improvement actions. This is a testable product hypothesis, **not** a claim that the product makes someone fit for public office.

### 1.4 Product principles

1. **Practice before performance theatre:** Judge reasoning and follow-through, not visibility, badges, or aspiration.
2. **Evidence before assertion:** Cite accessible sources; clearly separate fact, assumption, simulation, and opinion.
3. **Ethics by design:** Respect human rights, privacy, lawful authority, transparency, and plural legitimate interests.
4. **Challenge rather than flattery:** Identify weak assumptions, missing evidence, trade-offs, and second-order effects.
5. **Continuous improvement:** Every meaningful weakness may become an assigned learning or practice action.
6. **Separation of purposes:** Training, real-world service records, and personal political/career planning are independent data domains.
7. **No universal readiness score:** Display a multidimensional development profile and the quality of supporting evidence.

## 2. Users, Jobs to Be Done, and Positioning

### 2.1 Primary user

A motivated adult preparing for complex public, nonprofit, or institutional leadership who wants to practise executive judgment consistently and maintain a private, auditable learning record. The pilot may start with a single highly engaged user; the design must avoid assumptions that apply only to one person's office ambitions.

### 2.2 Secondary users (later releases)

- **Independent reviewer / mentor:** Reviews selected decisions or evidence by invitation, provides signed feedback, and cannot access the rest of the private workspace.
- **Scenario editor / quality reviewer:** Curates source-based cases, rubrics, and content versions; does not access user journals unless explicitly authorized.
- **Institutional learner:** Completes approved leadership exercises under an organization-specific program, subject to a distinct consent and data model.

### 2.3 Jobs to be done

- **When** I confront a difficult public decision, **I want** a realistic, constrained exercise **so that** I practise weighing evidence and competing interests.
- **When** I state a position, **I want** disciplined challenges **so that** I see assumptions, legal limits, overlooked groups, and implementation weaknesses.
- **When** I finish an exercise, **I want** grounded feedback and a clear next action **so that** my development remains measurable.
- **When** I revisit prior reasoning, **I want** to compare predictions and known developments **so that** I can detect miscalibration without confusing luck with skill.
- **When** I choose to share work with a mentor, **I want** granular permissions **so that** my private journal remains private.

### 2.4 Positioning

**Public description:** “A private practice system for serious public leadership.”  
**One-line promise:** “Make better decisions. Examine your assumptions. Prove your learning.”  
**Brand note:** STATESMAN OS is the working name; consider a more inclusive and less office-specific brand in user testing. Do not make presidential candidacy the public-facing identity.

## 3. Scope, Boundaries and Release Strategy

### 3.1 MVP: the smallest complete learning loop

| Priority | Capability | MVP result |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Decision Desk | One sourced scenario at a time with defined decision task and constraints |
| P0 | Decision Journal | Structured submission of options, rationale, assumptions, risks, confidence and expected outcomes |
| P0 | Challenge Review | Transparent challenges from legal, financial, ethical, operational and community viewpoints |
| P0 | Development Plan | User accepts/edits learning actions, assigns dates, and records completion evidence |
| P0 | Private workspace and review history | Secure sign-in, history, editable drafts, immutable submitted versions and export |
| P1 | Monthly review summary | Trend of completed decisions, unsupported assumptions and corrective actions |
| P1 | Curated Guyana-first case library | Pre-reviewed cases, source dates, and explicit simulation labels |

**MVP rule:** No full National Systems Atlas, advanced crisis engine, political pathway studio, public-service verification workflow, live news autopublishing, or composite “presidential readiness” score.

### 3.2 Future scope, conditional on evidence from MVP

| Phase | Capabilities | Gate before implementation |
|---|---|---|
| V1.1 | More case topics, guided weekly deep dive, reviewer invitation, 30/90-day retrospective prompts | Reliable weekly use, good case quality, safe reviewer permissions |
| V2 | Crisis Room, Virtual Cabinet with expanded perspectives, Policy Lab, competency evidence profile | Rubrics and challenge system validated against reviewer samples |
| V3 | Public Service Ledger with baseline/outcomes and independent verification; National Systems Atlas | Robust privacy, source verification and data governance |
| V4 | Private Strategic Pathway Studio for institutional experience, legal checkpoints and alternative careers | Separate governance charter, legal review and clear user demand |

**Release gating principle:** Advance phases because observed users successfully complete and return to the core loop, not because feature lists look impressive.

## 4. Core Learning Model — The STATESMAN Loop

| Step | User action | System output / quality control |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Encounter | Read a sourced scenario and decision task | Jurisdiction, as-of date, information gaps, timeframe, constraints and source access |
| 2. Decide | Write a chosen course of action | Locked response snapshot after submission; alternatives, trade-offs and confidence retained |
| 3. Defend | Answer perspective-based challenges | Each challenge identifies its basis, affected assumption and known uncertainty |
| 4. Observe | Review simulated outcome or later verified events | Explicitly labeled simulation; no fabricated real-world outcomes |
| 5. Reflect | Review rubric-based, explainable feedback | Distinguishes reasoning process from outcome and uncontrollable events |
| 6. Improve | Adopt corrective learning/practice action | Owner, due date, completion evidence and reassessment opportunity |

### 4.1 Decision lifecycle

**Scenario available → Draft response → Submitted (version locked) → Challenged → Reflection completed → Actions agreed → Reviewed/closed.** An exercise may be resumed while in draft. A submitted decision cannot be overwritten: the user may append a new revision, and both versions remain traceable. A retrospective may be “pending external evidence” without blocking completion of the learning exercise.

### 4.2 Intended learning cadence

- **Weekdays:** One focused decision exercise designed for approximately 15 minutes.
- **Weekly:** One optional deeper scenario with extended reasoning and implementation planning.
- **Monthly:** One formal review of evidence, recurring weaknesses and completed actions.
- **Quarterly (future/optional):** At least one independently reviewed exercise or project record.

These are **recommended practices**, not forced streaks. The experience should avoid punitive streaks and avoid substituting time spent for competence.

## 5. Product Experience and Screen Specifications

### 5.1 Screen A — Today

**Primary element:** Today's Decision, with sector, source date, estimated effort, time constraint and one unambiguous prompt. Primary action: **Start decision**. Secondary content: open development actions and recent factual feedback, not a large “readiness score”.

Illustrative scenario text: “Two regions face a suspected dengue outbreak. Testing capacity is limited, data are incomplete, and public confidence is weakening. Set first 72-hour priorities, identify additional information required, propose resource allocation and outline responsible public communication.” This is a **fictional training example**, not a claim of a current event.

### 5.2 Screen B — Decision Desk

Display source-grounded facts and constraints first. Use expandable panels for stakeholder positions and source documents. Prompt structured responses in this order: objective, options considered, recommended action, lawful authority to verify, resources/budget, stakeholders and equity, risks, uncertainty, confidence (0–100%), expected outcomes and indicators. Provide **Save draft** and **Submit decision**. Require explicit confirmation that submitted responses become a versioned record.

### 5.3 Screen C — Challenge Review

After submission, present perspective cards: **Legal authority**, **Public finance**, **Ethics and equity**, **Execution**, **Community impact**. Each card contains one or two prioritized challenges, the quoted decision passage or assumption being challenged, a short explanation, and an optional user response field. Show “Evidence insufficient” when the challenge cannot be grounded reliably. Never pretend to speak on behalf of an actual minister, institution or community.

### 5.4 Screen D — Reflection and Development

Present a five-dimension rubric profile, each with an evidence level and a confidence indicator. Show strengths, issues needing attention and an explicit distinction between **AI formative feedback** and **human validation**. The user selects one or two corrective actions, selects a reasonable review date, and may save a revised response with links to the initial version.

### 5.5 Screen E — Review History

List decisions by topic, date, state, sources, confidence and review status. Each item must open the full evidence trail: original case snapshot; submitted response; challenges; accepted/rejected feedback; reflective notes; actions; and later verified developments if any. Provide search, filter, export and delete functionality under the retention policy.

### 5.6 Screen F — Monthly Review (P1)

Show count of completed decisions, reviewed decisions, identified unsupported assumptions, completed corrective actions and examples of qualitative improvement. Compare like-for-like rubric assessments where possible. The user creates a monthly management-review record: achievements, persistent gaps, priority decisions and agreed next actions.

## 7. Competency and Evaluation Framework

### 7.1 Five assessment dimensions

| Dimension | What good evidence demonstrates |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | Accurate understanding of institutions, context, evidence and relevant uncertainty |
| Judgment | Prioritization under constraints; alternatives, trade-offs and second-order effects |
| Execution | Practical sequencing, roles, resources, indicators and implementation controls |
| Ethics | Legality, fairness, affected populations, transparency and accountability |
| Learning | Honest error recognition, adaptation, corrective actions and later reassessment |

### 7.2 Maturity levels per dimension

**0 Unassessed** — Insufficient evidence.  
**1 Understood** — Explains relevant concepts correctly.  
**2 Applied in exercises** — Demonstrates relevant application in structured scenarios.  
**3 Demonstrated in practice** — Documents relevant real-world performance and outcomes.  
**4 Independently validated** — A qualified external reviewer confirms adequate, repeatable evidence.

A simulation alone **cannot** establish level 3 or level 4. Scores are not additive into a presidential fitness index. Display observed level **plus** evidence quality (high / medium / low / insufficient), source, assessor and review date. No level progression occurs solely because an AI says performance has improved.

### 7.3 Formative decision-quality rubric

For each exercise, the AI may give provisional criterion-level comments against a pre-authored rubric: clarity of problem definition; use of evidence; feasibility and trade-offs; recognition of legal/ethical constraints; contingency planning; monitoring and learning. These comments describe the **submitted reasoning**, not the user's character or eligibility for office. Human reviewers can correct ratings and record justification.

### 7.4 Decision calibration

Capture forecast, confidence, deadline and later observation separately. Do not mark a case “right” merely because a favourable result occurred. Where no reliable observation is available, record **Not yet observable**. Aggregate trend comparisons require comparable scenario difficulty and reviewer calibration; otherwise label as descriptive only.

## 8. Content, Sourcing and Simulation Design

### 8.1 Scenario specification

Every approved case must store: case ID and version; learning objective; jurisdiction and sector; issue statement; user role; source list and access dates; fictional assumptions; decision deadline/window; available resources; unknown/conflicting facts; legitimate stakeholders; legal and budgetary constraints; possible second-order effects; author/editor approval; expected evaluation rubric; and publication/review status.

### 8.2 Editorial process

**Draft → Source verification → Subject-matter/ethics review → Approved → Published → Periodic refresh or archive.** MVP scenario content should be curated rather than generated from unreviewed live headlines. Content is dated and must not be described as current when freshness cannot be verified.

### 8.3 Distinguish outcomes

- **Simulated consequence:** A teaching-device result calculated or narrated within an exercise; never an empirical forecast.
- **Observed development:** A real event linked to dated, accessible evidence; users may compare it with earlier predictions.
- **Unobserved outcome:** No trustworthy follow-up evidence, or event too early to assess; show that limitation prominently.

The platform must explain that simulated outcomes cannot establish what would have happened under another policy choice in the real world.

## 9. AI Challenge System

### 9.1 AI roles

- **Case interpreter:** Summarizes only approved scenario material and marks uncertainty.
- **Constructive challenger:** Critically tests submitted reasoning using specified professional lenses.
- **Formative evaluator:** Maps passages to the rubric and returns justified, revisable observations.
- **Learning coach:** Suggests bounded, actionable learning exercises linked to diagnosed gaps.

### 9.2 Minimum response contract

Each generated challenge must return: perspective; concise question; targeted submitted text or assumption; relevant approved evidence reference (if applicable); uncertainty level; likely consequence or risk pathway; and category (**fact / inference / hypothetical / value judgment**). If the evidence does not support an answer, the model must abstain or pose the uncertainty as a question.

### 9.3 Human oversight and controls

All AI feedback is labeled **AI-generated formative analysis**. A prompt cannot assign an independently validated maturity level, assert eligibility for office, or impersonate real public officials. Users can flag errors, appeal interpretations and access the source material behind the claim. Evaluate the model with curated cases and reviewer comparisons before deployment and after material model changes.

### 9.4 AI safety and political boundaries

Prohibit voter profiling; sensitive political persuasion; targeted political messaging; opinion manipulation; fabrication of endorsements, legal authorities or events; claims of constitutional/legal certainty; covert publication of private journals; and presentation of simulated consequences as actual forecasts. Later career planning must remain private and informational, not a political campaign operation.

## 10. Data Model, Permissions and Retention

### 10.1 Primary entities

| Entity | Representative fields |
|---|---|
| User | User ID, settings, access controls, locale, created date |
| Scenario | ID, version, status, sector, jurisdiction, tasks, assumptions, source refs, rubric ID |
| Source | Title, publisher, URI, publication date, retrieved date, factual claim mappings, confidence |
| Decision | ID, user, scenario version, draft state, submitted timestamp, objectives/options/actions, confidence, forecasts |
| DecisionRevision | Parent ID, version number, content snapshot, created timestamp, rationale for change |
| Challenge | Decision version, perspective, challenged passage, evidence, uncertainty, generation version, user response |
| Assessment | Criteria, observations, provisional AI analysis, evidence rating, reviewer ID/status, date |
| DevelopmentAction | Gap, action description, owner, due date, status, completion evidence, linked reassessment |
| ReviewRecord | Period, evidence snapshot, conclusions, agreed actions, reviewer metadata |
| AuditEvent | Actor, action, entity ID, timestamp, non-sensitive change metadata |

### 10.2 Access principles

MVP user is the only default reader of private development records. Editor access to scenario content does not convey journal access. Later reviewer roles must be explicitly invited to scoped records, time-limited where feasible, with revocation. Public-service evidence, if eventually published, requires an independent publication consent flow; raw private notes are never automatically included.

### 10.3 Data lifecycle

Drafts can be edited; submitted records are versioned; completion and reviews are linked rather than silently rewriting history. Implement documented export, account deletion and backups. Minimize personal and third-party data; never upload confidential government, patient, constituent or employer records to the AI service without authorization and applicable safeguards.

## 12. Success Metrics and Research Plan

### 12.1 Primary product metric

**Meaningful learning loops completed:** Number and rate of decisions that include a submitted rationale, at least one addressed challenge, a reflection, and either an accepted improvement action or reasoned decision that no action is needed. Exclude exercises abandoned after the first screen.

### 12.2 Supporting pilot measures (provisional targets)

| Metric | Definition | Initial pilot threshold for discussion |
|---|---|---|
| Week-4 retention | Proportion of pilot users completing >=1 meaningful loop in week 4 | >= 60% |
| Weekly practice | Proportion completing >=3 meaningful loops in a given active week | >= 50% |
| Reflection completion | Submitted decisions with a completed reflection | >= 80% |
| Action follow-through | Due improvement actions completed with evidence | >= 60% |
| Source integrity | Published factual case assertions with traceable source or simulation label | 100% |
| Unsafe AI assertions | Critical failures in audited challenge responses | 0 critical failures in release test set |
| Reviewer usefulness | Reviewer ratings that challenges identify meaningful blind spots | >= 75% favorable |

These are **hypotheses for pilot validation**, not forecasts. Do not claim genuine capability improvement from retention or self-ratings alone; test rubric reliability with independent reviewers and comparable exercises.

### 12.3 Research protocol

Start with a private single-user dogfood pilot, then a small, consent-based pilot across varied civic/institutional backgrounds. Collect task completion friction, perceived relevance, challenge usefulness, source accuracy, response quality, and examples of changed reasoning. Hold a product review after the first month; change the rubric/content before expanding feature scope if reviewers find evaluation unreliable.

## 13. Risks, Controls and Dependencies

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation / owner to assign |
|---|---|---|
| Hallucinated facts or legal claims | False learning and misplaced confidence | Curated sources, verified claims, explicit uncertainty and human audit |
| AI feedback reinforces user bias | Inflated confidence / missed weaknesses | Diverse challenge lenses, disagreement workflow, reviewer calibration |
| Engagement without actual improvement | Attractive but hollow product | Completion metrics plus independent reasoning-quality checks |
| Scenario staleness | Misleading case context | Publish as-of date, review cadence, archival and source-status check |
| Sensitive material leakage | Privacy or professional harm | Data minimization, encryption, separate roles, no automatic publishing |
| Office/campaign branding | Reduced trust, inappropriate use | Neutral leadership positioning; political-pathway functions deferred |
| MVP scope expansion | Delayed learning and greater cost | Four-element core scope with explicit release gates |
| Overdependence on a single AI vendor | Cost, availability and behavior shifts | Abstract model interface, versioned prompts, regression tests, fallback |

**Dependencies:** approved editorial rubric; initial curated case set; secure authentication and storage; AI provider/API; privacy and legal review; a test panel for scenario quality; developer and QA capacity.

## 14. MVP Delivery Plan and Release Gates

### 14.1 Sequence of implementation

| Workstream | Output | Dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and rules | Final journeys, scoring rubric, ethics charter and data classification | Product owner + qualified reviewers |
| Content foundation | Initial 10–15 reviewed cases across sectors, with source registers | Editorial and subject-matter review |
| Core application | Today, Decision Desk, Journal and immutable submissions | Secure auth and versioned data schema |
| AI integration | Challenge Review with evidence links, error handling and evaluation harness | Approved case/rubric definitions |
| Improvement engine | Development actions and review history | Decision and challenge lineage |
| Pilot verification | Accessibility/security/source tests and reviewer-assisted user testing | Stable end-to-end learning loop |

### 14.2 Go / no-go conditions

Release only if: (a) a pilot user can complete a full loop without assistance; (b) submitted decisions remain immutable and traceable; (c) all required citations and simulation labels are reliable; (d) no critical authorization flaw or critical unsafe AI assertion remains open; (e) user can recover from interrupted sessions; and (f) a reviewer can explain and reproduce the intended scoring logic.

### 14.3 Out-of-scope launch requests

Defer leaderboards, arbitrary XP, popularity scores, social feeds, political targeting, public profiles, real-time electoral advice, full ministry maps, automatic news-based scenario creation and third-party evidence publication. They are not needed to test the core hypothesis.

## Appendix A — Worked MVP Scenario Template

**Case ID:** DEMO-PH-001  
**Title:** A Regional Dengue Response Under Uncertainty  
**Status:** Fictional illustrative content only; not an actual current public-health briefing  
**Time window:** First 72 hours  
**User role:** National coordinating executive for a simulated interagency response  
**Constraints:** Limited testing capacity, incomplete surveillance data, finite transport, conflicting local reports  
**Required decision:** Define priorities, identify the decision authority requiring verification, allocate resources, specify communication approach and safeguards for vulnerable groups.

**Journal form:** (1) problem objective; (2) three alternatives; (3) selected action and rationale; (4) assumptions and missing data; (5) operational roles/resources; (6) equity/legal checks; (7) confidence level; (8) indicators and forecast.

**Challenge examples:**

- **Legal:** “Which legal power authorizes the proposed restriction, and what must be verified before using it?”
- **Financial:** “What service is delayed when funding is reassigned to testing, and is the trade-off justified?”
- **Ethical:** “How will your plan avoid disadvantaging communities with limited transport or access to clinics?”
- **Operational:** “What is the fallback if test results are delayed by 48 hours?”
- **Community:** “How will you communicate uncertainty without either minimising the risk or causing unnecessary alarm?”

**Reflection example:** Record a specific missing assumption (e.g., transport capacity is not confirmed), link it to a development action (“Prepare a two-page interagency resource-verification checklist”), and set a date to repeat a comparable scenario. None of these challenge questions imply the existence of actual statutory authority or current outbreak statistics.
